🧭 What a System Design Interview Tests

💡 Break a vague, big problem into infra pieces. There's no single right answer. You're judged on 4 things, and Problem Navigation matters most.

  • 🧠 Vague problem → infra pieces: services, load balancers, databases.
  • 🛠️ Practical, closer to real work than a LeetCode round.
  • 🎯 Many right answers. They judge how you navigate, weigh trade-offs, and communicate.
  • ⚠️ Going deeper than an interview needs can be a yellow flag.

🛤️ Delivery Framework

💡 #1 reason mid-level candidates fail: no working system (it gets called "time management"). Follow 6 steps in order. Build simple first, then harden.

  • 🎯 It's not about going twice as fast. It's about focusing on the right things.
  • 🧯 Nervous or lost? The steps are your path to fall back on.

⏱️ The 6 steps · tap one to open it

Steps 1–5 · goal: meet the functional requirements

1📋 Requirements~5 min

🎯 Functional: "Users should be able to…" Pick the top 3. Ask like a PM: "does it need X?", "what if Y?"

🐦 post tweets➕ follow users📰 see tweets of people they follow

⚠️ A long list hurts. Top FAANG companies grade you on focusing on what matters.

⚙️ Non-functional: "The system should be…" Pick the top 3–5. Put them in context and use numbers.

❌ "low latency"
✅ "search < 500ms"
🐦 availability > consistency100M+ DAUfeed < 200ms

🧠 Checklist, remember it as FCC + SLEDS (from the comments):

Fault toleranceCAPComplianceScalabilityLatencyEnvironmentDurabilitySecurity
🔀 CAPConsistency or availability? (Partition tolerance is a given.)
📈 ScaleBursty traffic? Read-heavy or write-heavy?
⚡ LatencyThe heavy requests, e.g. Yelp search
💾 DurabilitySocial feed can lose some · bank can't
📱 EnvironmentBattery, memory, bandwidth (video on 3G)

🧮 Capacity estimation: ⏭️ skip it upfront. Do the math only when it changes the design.

❌ DAU, QPS, storage → "ok, it's a lot"
✅ TopK trending: number of topics → one min-heap or sharded?
2🧱 Core Entities~2 min

📝 A bulleted list, said out loud as a first draft. These are the nouns your API exchanges and your DB stores.

👤 User🐦 Tweet➕ Follow

❓ Who are the actors? What nouns do the functional requirements need?

🚫 No full data model yet ("you don't know what you don't know"). Fields come later, in the high-level design.

🏷️ Pick good names. Some interviewers check this.

3🔌 API / Interface~5 min
⭐ RESTDefault. HTTP verbs on resources
GraphQLMany clients needing different data
RPC (gRPC)Internal service-to-service, when speed matters
WS / SSEReal-time, added after the core API
POST /v1/tweets      {text}
GET  /v1/tweets/{id}  → Tweet
POST /v1/follows     {followee_id}
GET  /v1/feed        → Tweet[]

📦 Plural nouns: tweets, not tweet. Usually maps 1:1 to the functional requirements (not always).

🔐 The current user comes from the auth token, never from the body or path.

4🌊 Data Flow (optional)~5 min

⏭️ Only for data-processing systems with a long sequence of actions. Otherwise skip it. Write it as a simple list.

🕷️ Web crawler:Fetch seed URLs→Parse HTML→Extract URLs→Store data→🔁 Repeat
5🏗️ High-level Design~10–15 min

📦 Boxes and arrows that satisfy your API. Build it one endpoint at a time 👇

📱 Client→🚪 API Gateway
✏️ Tweet CRUD Service→🗄️ Tweet DB (Postgres)id · authorId · text · media: s3Links[]
👥 Follower Service→🗄️ Following DB (Postgres)userId1 · userId2
📰 Feed Service→① getFollowers()
② getTweets(followers)

🪶 Keep it simple and meet the functional requirements. Spot a cache or a queue? Say it, write a note, move on.

🗣️ Talk while you draw: how data flows, and what state changes on each request.

🗄️ Write the key fields next to the DB. Skip the obvious ones (name, email) and the types.

🖊️ Ask your recruiter which whiteboard tool you'll use (e.g. Excalidraw) and practice with it.

Step 6 · goal: meet the non-functional requirements

6🔬 Deep Dives~10 min

🔨 Harden the design:

✅ meet the non-functional requirements🧩 edge cases🚧 bottlenecks🎯 interviewer probes
100M+ DAU→ horizontal scaling · caches · DB sharding
Feed < 200ms→ fanout-on-read vs fanout-on-write + caches (the most interesting part of Twitter)

⚠️ Don't talk over the interviewer. Leave room for their probes: they're looking for specific signals, and it counts toward communication.

  • 🎤 Say it out loud: "I'll skip estimates and do the math when it changes the design."
  • 🎤 In the high-level design, call out "this feed will be slow" → fix it in Deep Dives.
