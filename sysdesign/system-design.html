🧭 What a System Design Interview Tests

💡 Break a vague, big problem into infra pieces. There's no single right answer. You're judged on 4 things, and Problem Navigation matters most.

  • 🧠 Vague problem → infra pieces: services, load balancers, databases.
  • 🛠️ Practical, closer to real work than a LeetCode round.
  • 🎯 Many right answers. They judge how you navigate, weigh trade-offs, and communicate.
  • 📈 Rare at entry level · common at mid · the norm at senior, where it weighs heavily.
  • ⚖️ Mid-level: cover the basics well. Senior: basics fast, then show depth in deep dives.
  • ⚠️ Going deeper than an interview needs can be a yellow flag.

🧭 Problem Navigation
⭐ Most important · where most struggle
  • Skip requirements
  • Polish trivial parts
  • Get stuck
  • No working system
They spent all their time on the User table, never touching the most important part!
🧩 Solution Design
  • Weak core concepts
  • Ignore scale
  • Spaghetti design
The multi-layer cache was an elegant solution to the massive read volume
⚙️ Technical Excellence
  • Don't know the tech
  • Think it's still 2015
  • Can't apply the tech
  • Miss common patterns
They had strong command of Redis, Elasticsearch, and Inverted Indexes
🗣️ Communication
  • Unclear explanations
  • Defensive on feedback
  • Lost in the weeds
They got quite defensive when I asked about alternative approaches
  • 🎤 #1 cause of failure = no structure → follow the Delivery Framework.
  • 🤔 They probe, doubt you, and ask "why not X?" to catch memorised answers → answer with trade-offs, don't defend.
  • 🏋️ Practice beats just reading: people who only read stumble on the day.
🔬 Details
📈 Levels
  • Entry level: usually no SD round, with plenty of exceptions.
  • Staff+: a different interview, so adjust your approach (HelloInterview "Staff-Level System Design" blog).
  • Each problem breakdown in the course lists what's expected at each level.
📋 Rubric
  • Every company has its own rubric and wording, but the same 4 themes.
  • All levels must finish a full design that meets the requirements.
  • Weak navigation = time burned on the wrong problems + a poor impression.
  • Much SD material is stuck in 2015. The course flags outdated approaches, see 📏 Numbers to Know.
🗺️ Not your interview type?
  • Class structure (LLD / OOD) → "Low-Level Design in a Hurry".
  • ML modelling / features → "ML System Design in a Hurry".
  • Frontend → GreatFrontend.
  • Real asked questions: HelloInterview community questions.
🎓 How the course works
  • Built backwards from interview skills: Delivery Framework → core concepts → full problems.
  • Why: you always know what's next, and you link ideas to real systems.
  • Do the required steps in order. Early chapters: video + written guide + quiz. Later chapters: try a real problem → review the solution → learn the pattern.
  • Optional video reviews don't count toward completion.
  • ≈ 28 h total (video, reading, quizzes, Guided Practice), usually spread over 2–4 months. Your pace is a target, not a deadline.
